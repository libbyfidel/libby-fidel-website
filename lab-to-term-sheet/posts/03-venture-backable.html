Is it venture-backable?

Nine questions every VC asks silently in the first meeting. Answer them honestly before anyone else does.

4 min read · 3 min quizInteractiveFundraising · Self-assessment

A company can be important, technically brilliant, and profitable, and still be a bad fit for venture capital. That isn't a judgment of the work. It's a mismatch between the company's shape and what a fund's math requires (see Part 2).

Here are the nine criteria I see investors use over and over, grouped into three questions: Can it get big? Is it real? Can it last? Rate yourself on each one. "Not yet" is a perfectly good answer at the early stage. What matters is knowing which ones they are.

If you scored low

Good. Now you know. Many of the most durable hard-tech companies aren't built on venture money at all. They're built on grants, strategic partners, project finance, or customer revenue. Picking the wrong capital is one of the most expensive mistakes a technical founder can make, because it signs you up for a growth curve your technology may not support.

Part 5 covers the alternatives. And if your gaps sit in market or customer pain, Part 4 is a tool for finding a bigger, more urgent problem your technology could solve.

Takeaways
  • Venture fit comes down to scale, reality, and durability.
  • At the early stage, "not yet" is fine, but know your plan to get to yes.
  • Not venture-backable ≠ not fundable. Match the capital to the company.
