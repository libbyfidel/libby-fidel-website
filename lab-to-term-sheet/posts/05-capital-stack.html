Before you raise, build a capital stack

Venture is one tool, not the whole toolbox. Mix funding sources so that, when you do take a VC check, it's on your terms.

6 min readInteractiveFundraising · Strategy

It's tempting to raise as much venture capital as you can, as fast as you can. For hard-tech companies that's often a mistake. Each equity round buys speed, and you pay for it with ownership and a growth curve someone else has set.

The best technical founders I know treat funding as a stack: they layer cheaper, non-dilutive capital under the equity, and use each kind for the job it does best.

When the market doesn't exist yet

Whatever money you go after, someone will ask how big the market is. For new technologies the honest answer is often "small today." That's fine, as long as you can show how it gets big. There are four common ways to make that case:

Show the market is larger than it first looks: the growth rate, new geographies, and adjacent segments that reach the same buyer. Start with credible industry data and narrow down to the part you can actually serve.
Build up from the units: number of target customers × what they would pay × realistic share. Investors usually trust this more than top-down numbers because every assumption can be checked. Back each one with interviews or public data.
Lay out the sequence: a beachhead market you can win now, and the milestones that unlock bigger, higher-value markets later. Your why-ladder from Part 4 is often the map.
Attach yourself to a big shift investors already believe in (electrification, AI compute demand, reshoring, decarbonization) and show why that shift gives you unusually high share or pricing power.
Takeaways
  • Use non-dilutive capital to de-risk the technology; use equity to scale it.
  • Early dilution compounds. The cheapest dollars are the first ones.
  • For new markets, sell the path to big, not today's size.
